# 08 — Portfolio and Reel Evaluation Framework

**Purpose:** Replace "I know it when I see it" with a defensible, repeatable evaluation that still leaves room for human judgment.
**Applies to:** Photography, Videography, Content & Social, Design & Creative.
**Status:** `PROPOSED` operating standard.

---

## 1. Design principles

1. **Evidence before opinion.** Every score has a link, timestamp, and one-line observation.
2. **Six criteria, fixed weights.** Consistency matters more than a long list.
3. **Score the work, not the person.** No inference about personality, background, or "culture fit."
4. **Separate floor from ranking.** A candidate can pass the floor and still not be the best fit for a specific brief.
5. **Calibrate regularly.** Two reviewers should land within 20 points on the same portfolio.
6. **Human owns the outcome.** The rubric informs; it does not decide.

---

## 2. Scoring mechanics

- Each criterion scored **1–5**.
- Weighted total normalized to **0–100**.
- `score = Σ (criterion_score / 5 × weight) × 100`, where weights sum to 100.
- Record `evidence_completeness` (0–100%) separately. A high score on thin evidence is not a high-confidence score.
- Bands:

| Band | Range | Meaning | Default action |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 85–100 | Exceptional; exceeds role bar | Fast-track review; interview |
| B | 70–84 | Strong; meets role bar | Interview if brief fit |
| C | 55–69 | Mixed; meets some criteria | Hold or second review |
| D | 40–54 | Below role bar | Decline unless evidence incomplete |
| F | 0–39 | Clearly below bar | Decline |

**Hard rule:** a candidate with `evidence_completeness < 60%` cannot be declined on score alone. Request evidence first.

**Vertical floors (proposal):**

| Vertical | Floor to advance | Strong signal |
|---|---|---|
| Photography | 65 | 80+ |
| Videography | 68 | 82+ |
| Content & Social | 65 | 80+ |
| Design & Creative | 68 | 82+ |

Floors are reviewed quarterly against actual performance. They are not quotas.

## 3. Photography rubric

| Criterion | Weight | 1 — Below bar | 3 — Meets bar | 5 — Exceeds bar | Evidence to capture |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Composition | 20 | Cropping distracts; no focal point | Clear subject and framing | Intentional negative space, layered frames, consistent point of view | 3 example images |
| Lighting | 20 | Flat or blown; inconsistent color | Controlled natural or artificial light | Sculpts light; handles mixed color and harsh conditions | Before/after or set notes |
| Retouching | 15 | Over-processed or obvious artifacts | Clean, natural skin and color | Invisible retouching; consistent across set | 100% crops |
| Consistency | 15 | Set feels like different shooters | Coherent look across the set | A repeatable visual system across projects | 10-image sequence |
| Brief fit | 20 | Ignores the stated use case | Meets the brief | Adds a clear idea without breaking the brief | Brief + selected frames |
| Range | 10 | One lighting/style only | Two or three modes | Editorial, product, event, portrait with equal confidence | 3 distinct projects |

**Photography evidence notes:**

- Prefer a full set over a highlight reel. A set reveals consistency.
- Ask for unedited or lightly edited frames if retouching is a concern.
- Check licensing: can the agency show the work?

## 4. Videography rubric

| Criterion | Weight | 1 — Below bar | 3 — Meets bar | 5 — Exceeds bar | Evidence to capture |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Storytelling | 25 | No arc; disconnected shots | Clear beginning/middle/end | Emotional or conceptual through-line; strong pacing | Full piece + reel |
| Camera | 15 | Unstable, poorly exposed | Controlled, intentional framing | Distinct visual language; confident movement | 60-second segment |
| Sound | 15 | Unusable audio; abrupt levels | Clean dialogue/ambience | Designed sound; music and mix support story | Headphones check |
| Edit | 20 | Choppy; unclear transitions | Clean continuity | Rhythm, restraint, surprise; cut matches intent | Full piece |
| Color | 10 | Inconsistent or heavy-handed | Coherent grade | Grade serves mood and brand | Before/after stills |
| Pacing / runtime | 15 | Too long or rushed | Fits format | Cutdowns work natively for each platform | 1:1, 9:16, 16:9 versions |

**Videography evidence notes:**

- A reel shows range; a full piece shows judgment. Require both.
- Confirm the candidate's role on each piece (DP, editor, colorist, director).
- Check music licensing and whether the piece can be shared.

## 5. Content & Social rubric

| Criterion | Weight | 1 — Below bar | 3 — Meets bar | 5 — Exceeds bar | Evidence to capture |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Platform fluency | 20 | Generic cross-posts | Native format per platform | Uses platform mechanics to change behavior | 3 platform examples |
| Copy | 20 | Generic, error-prone | Clear, on-brand | Distinct voice; hooks and CTAs that fit | 5 captions + rationale |
| Visual consistency | 15 | Inconsistent grid/typography | Coherent look | A recognizable system across formats | 9-post grid |
| Analytics literacy | 15 | Cannot explain results | Reads reach/engagement | Connects content decisions to outcomes | 1 report + interpretation |
| Brand safety | 15 | Tone-deaf or risky | Follows guidelines | Anticipates risk and proposes guardrails | 1 tricky scenario |
| Cadence / operations | 15 | No calendar | Can run a calendar | Builds repeatable calendar and approval flow | 2-week calendar |

**Content evidence notes:**

- Ask for a sample calendar, not just finished posts.
- Ask what they would change based on the metrics.
- Check whether they can write in more than one brand voice.

## 6. Design & Creative rubric

| Criterion | Weight | 1 — Below bar | 3 — Meets bar | 5 — Exceeds bar | Evidence to capture |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Concept | 20 | Decoration without idea | Clear idea | Original, strategic idea that solves the brief | 2 case studies |
| Craft | 20 | Misalignment, poor spacing | Clean execution | Precise typography, grid, and detail | 100% crops |
| Typography | 15 | Default fonts, poor hierarchy | Clear hierarchy | Expressive, controlled type system | Type specimen |
| Systems thinking | 15 | One-off artifacts | Repeatable components | Scalable system across touchpoints | Brand/system example |
| File hygiene | 15 | Flattened, unnamed files | Organized layers | Named, linked, print-ready, accessible | Source file walkthrough |
| Brief fit | 15 | Ignores constraints | Meets constraints | Turns constraints into an advantage | Brief + outcome |

**Design evidence notes:**

- Require a source-file walkthrough for at least one project.
- Check print specs if print work is claimed.
- Check accessibility (contrast, alt text, reading order) for digital work.
- Confirm font licensing.

## 7. Evidence completeness scoring

| Evidence element | Weight |
|---|---|
| Portfolio/reel accessible | 25 |
| 3+ relevant pieces in the target vertical | 20 |
| Full piece or full set (not only highlights) | 20 |
| Candidate's role stated per piece | 15 |
| Process/rationale available (case study, notes, walkthrough) | 10 |
| Rights/permission to review | 10 |

`evidence_completeness = earned / 100`.

**Rules:**

- `< 60%`: request missing evidence; do not decline.
- `60–79%`: score, but flag `moderate confidence`.
- `≥ 80%`: score with `high confidence`.

---

## 8. Review procedure

1. **Open a clean session.** No candidate name visible until after scoring, where possible, to reduce bias.
2. **Review the full set once without scoring.** Note first impressions in one sentence.
3. **Score each criterion independently.** Do not compute a running total.
4. **Write evidence for each score.** Link to the exact piece.
5. **Compute the weighted total.**
6. **Write a 3–5 sentence summary:** strengths, gaps, and fit for the specific brief.
7. **Record confidence.**
8. **If below floor:** second reviewer before decline.
9. **If above floor:** route to interview or decision.

---

## 9. Calibration

### 9.1 Monthly calibration

- Select 3 portfolios already reviewed.
- Two reviewers rescore independently.
- Compare criterion by criterion.
- Discuss differences > 1 point on any criterion.
- Update rubric language if ambiguity is systemic.

### 9.2 Drift indicators

| Indicator | Threshold | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Mean score by reviewer differs > 10 points | 2 consecutive months | Coaching or rubric review |
| Approval rate by reviewer differs > 25% | 1 quarter | Review for bias |
| Same portfolio rescored > 15 points apart | Any | Mandatory calibration |
| `evidence_completeness` average < 70% | 1 month | Fix intake, not reviewers |

### 9.3 Bias safeguards

- Remove name, photo, location, and school before first-pass scoring where feasible.
- Score against the brief, not against the last candidate.
- Require a reason code for every decline.
- Review rejection patterns quarterly by reviewer and source.
- Never use "culture fit" as a criterion.

## 10. Worked examples

### 10.1 Photography — strong but narrow

Scores: composition 4, lighting 4, retouching 4, consistency 5, brief fit 3, range 2.

Weighted: `(4/5×20)+(4/5×20)+(4/5×15)+(5/5×15)+(3/5×20)+(2/5×10)`
= 16 + 16 + 12 + 15 + 12 + 4 = **75 (Band B)**.

Evidence: 12-image editorial set, consistent natural light, no flash work. Brief required flash for a evening event.

Decision: interview for editorial work; do not staff for the flash-heavy event without a test.

### 10.2 Videography — beautiful reel, unclear ownership

Scores: storytelling 4, camera 4, sound 3, edit 4, color 4, pacing 3.

Weighted: 20 + 12 + 9 + 16 + 8 + 9 = **74 (Band B)**.

Evidence completeness: 55% because the candidate did not state their role on two pieces and no full piece was provided.

Decision: **do not decline.** Request a full piece and role confirmation. Re-score after evidence arrives.

### 10.3 Design — strong craft, weak system

Scores: concept 4, craft 5, typography 4, systems 2, file hygiene 2, brief fit 4.

Weighted: 16 + 20 + 12 + 6 + 6 + 12 = **72 (Band B)**.

Decision: interview for campaign work; hold for a brand-system engagement until source-file and system evidence is provided.

---

## 11. Re-score and appeal

A candidate may request feedback. The agency may:

- provide the band and one improvement area (not the full internal scorecard),
- re-review if new evidence is supplied,
- correct an error.

A reviewer may request a second review if:

- evidence was incomplete,
- the brief was ambiguous,
- the score feels inconsistent with the evidence.

Second reviews are recorded, not erased. The final decision belongs to the human decision owner.

## 12. Output schema

```json
{
  "applicant_id": "string",
  "vertical": "photography | videography | content_social | design",
  "reviewer_id": "string",
  "reviewed_at": "ISO-8601",
  "evidence": [
    { "type": "portfolio|reel|file|case_study", "url": "string", "note": "string" }
  ],
  "criteria": [
    { "key": "composition", "score": 1, "weight": 20, "evidence_note": "string" }
  ],
  "weighted_score": 0,
  "band": "A|B|C|D|F",
  "evidence_completeness": 0,
  "confidence": "high|moderate|low",
  "summary": "string",
  "recommendation": "advance|hold|decline",
  "recommendation_reason": "string",
  "human_decision": null
}
```

The `human_decision` field is intentionally `null` until a named human records it. The assistant must never populate it.
